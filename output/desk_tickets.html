A single agent needs one loop per ticket and calls each distinct tool about once. Compare the "Agent loops" and "Tokens" columns with the "Distinct MCP tools" column.

Ticket 102 should be one agent (evidence)

The whole job is a straight line with no decisions branching between specialists:

  1. get_ticket(102)
  2. get_lease_rent_status(1)
  3. get_cash_position()
  4. request_payment_approval("lease", 1, …)
  5. update_ticket(…, "resolved")

One "ops" agent with those five MCP tools would do it in roughly 4–6 model requests. The team version starts a Boss loop plus a Facilities loop plus an Accounting loop. Each loads its own system prompt, many re-call get_ticket, and every hand-off re-states facts in prose. That's more tokens, more latency, and more places for a fact to get garbled.

Nothing about safety would be lost, because the guardrails live in the MCP tools, not in the agent split:

  • the amount comes from leases.monthly_rent
  • duplicates are refused
  • execute_approved_payment is human-only and refuses negative balances

Ticket 101 is mostly a fixed pipeline (evidence)

The Expected flow on the 101 tab is always Inventory → Accounting → Customer Service. The order is forced by data dependencies: you can't pay before you know the vendor is blocked, and you can't write the email before you know the plan. Fixed sequences are cheaper as one agent, or even as plain code calling the tools, with a single model call to write the customer draft. The "Boss decides" step added little here, because the database leaves no real choice.

Where the team did earn its cost: ticket 103 and the shared cash (103, Cash)

  • Separation of duties. Customer Service can't file a price override, and only Accounting can file money requests (see the tool allow-lists in harness.md §4). In a real shop, the person writing the friendly email shouldn't also be the one approving the discount.
  • Cross-ticket judgment. Ticket 103 needs Accounting to see that 101 already queued invoice 501, and that the $264 hoodie PO competes with 102's $2,400 rent for the same checking account. A specialist whose only job is cash catches that more reliably than one agent juggling pricing, stock and messaging in a single prompt.
  • Readable audits. The live feed and audit_trail.json show who decided what. That's useful when a human asks why a $49.30 price was proposed.

Verdict: use a single agent with all read tools plus role-checked write tools for routine tickets like 102 and most of 101. Bring in the Accounting specialist when cash is contested, as on 103, or when several tickets compete for one balance.

3 · Three new problems this team can solve with the tools already built

A. A customer orders 6 Crest Mugs (CC-MUG-CREST)

Why it fits: the inventory row exists with 0 on hand. The vendors table has Elm City Gifts (id 2, "mugs and small goods", 3 lead days), which has no open invoices, unlike Bulldog in 101. pricing has a unit cost of $3.50.

How the team solves it:

  1. Boss → Inventory: check_inventory_stock("CC-MUG-CREST","OS",6) gives a shortfall of 6. check_vendor_status() picks vendor 2 by specialty, with will_ship_new_product = true and arrival in 3 days.
  2. Boss → Accounting: request_purchase_order_approval(2,"CC-MUG-CREST","OS",6,…) for 6 × $3.50 = $21.00, prepaid.
  3. Boss → Customer Service: save_customer_draft with a real arrival date.

It's the 101 pattern without the blocker. It shows vendor choice by specialty working on a vendor the three tickets never touched. Even after the run's cash outflows on the Cash tab, a $21 PO fits.

B. Next month's rent arrives and the shop can't cover it

Why it fits: after ticket 102's payment, execute_approved_payment moved leases.next_due forward a month (see 102). The Cash tab shows how little checking is left, and no revenue is modeled.

How the team solves it:

  • Facilities verifies the new notice with get_lease_rent_status(1). covers_rent = false and a negative balance_after_rent make the gap explicit.
  • Accounting still files the request, and its warning says cash can't cover it.
  • The Boss marks the risk clearly for the human.

If anyone approves it anyway, execute_approved_payment refuses and nothing moves: no negative balance, which is a stated rule. The team solves the decision-support problem honestly: it tells the owner exactly how short they are. It can't solve the cash problem itself; see section 4, problem 1.

C. The Yale AI Club replies "we'll take the 8 now, ship the rest when you can"

Why it fits: it follows directly from 103. Stock of 8 size M is still on hand, and the approved or pending price override is on the board (list_approval_requests(ticket_id=103)).

How the team solves it:

  • Inventory reconfirms 8 on hand.
  • Accounting files a new price override for qty 8 at the same unit price (request_price_override_approval refuses anything below cost).
  • Accounting re-checks whether the 12-unit hoodie PO can be paid yet, using get_cash_position. If the Cash tab shows it was refused, Accounting re-files a smaller PO that fits, or recommends waiting.
  • Customer Service drafts the split-shipment confirmation.

4 · Three new problems this team cannot solve today, and what it would take

1. Money coming in: sales, deposits, refunds

The problem: the AI Club pays for its 20 hoodies, or Tauhid wants a refund because the tee arrives late.

Why the team can't: this homework models only cash out, as the Cash tab shows:

  • There is no sales or receivables table.
  • No tool can increase cash_accounts.
  • No tool decrements inventory.qty when a customer takes product.

That is also why scenario B can only warn, not fix.

What it needs:

  • Tables: sales, refunds, receivables.
  • MCP tools:
    • record_sale(ticket_id, sku, size, qty, unit_price), which reduces inventory and records revenue, enforcing any approved price override
    • record_customer_payment(...), which increases cash
    • request_refund_approval(...) plus a human-only execute_refund, which also refuses negative balances
    • a read-only payment-processor connector (for example Stripe) to match deposits
  • Agent: a Sales / Point-of-Sale agent that owns the order-to-cash flow. Accounting gains a reconciliation duty: payments in versus the processor.

2. Real procurement: receiving goods, vendor bills, finding another supplier

The problem: any of these:

  • the tee from 101 arrives on 2026-09-05 and must go on the shelf
  • Bulldog sends a real invoice
  • Bulldog is too expensive or blocked again, and the shop needs a second apparel printer to fill 103's 12 hoodies within the cash left on the Cash tab

Why the team can't:

  • place_approved_purchase_order deliberately doesn't add stock.
  • There's no "receive" step.
  • Vendors have no SKU catalog or prices; Inventory matches on a free-text specialty.
  • Only three vendors exist.
  • Agents are forbidden to contact anyone.

What it needs:

  • Tables: vendor_catalog(vendor_id, sku, unit_cost, min_qty, lead_days) and purchase_orders with a status life-cycle.
  • MCP tools:
    • receive_shipment(po_id, qty_received), which updates inventory and flags short shipments
    • record_vendor_invoice(...)
    • compare_vendor_quotes(sku, qty)
    • draft_vendor_message plus a human-only send_message, so outbound email only goes after approval
    • a web or supplier-search tool for sourcing
  • Agent: a Procurement agent that negotiates and sources. Inventory keeps on-hand truth.

3. Time, planning and forecasting

The problem: "Move-in week and the first home game are coming. How many navy hoodies and white tees should we print, and will we have cash for rent on 2026-10-02?"

Why the team can't:

  • Time is frozen at desk.date_today = 2026-08-31.
  • There is no sales history, no calendar of campus events and no scheduler.
  • Every tool answers "what is true today", never "what will be true".
  • All three tickets were reactive. Ticket 101's stock-out of size S tees (0 on hand) is exactly what forecasting would have prevented.

What it needs:

  • Tables: sales_history and events.
  • MCP tools:
    • advance_shop_date or a scheduler, so recurring rent creates its own ticket
    • forecast_demand(sku, size, horizon_days)
    • cash_forecast(days), combining expected sales, rent and open POs
    • set_reorder_point(sku, size, min_qty)
  • Agent: a Planning / Forecast agent that runs on a schedule and opens its own tickets, such as "reorder 15 × CC-TEE-WHITE S before 09-05". Accounting would review a 30-day cash forecast instead of a single balance.

Smaller gaps worth noting:

  • payroll and staff scheduling
  • sales-tax filing
  • fraud checks on vendor bank-detail changes
  • two-person approval above a dollar limit

Each would need its own table, tools and a human-only approval step, built the same way as execute_approved_payment.
